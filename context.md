# 2026 ASOC x Taylor Fry Actuarial Case Competition — Context

Source: `UNSW ASOC x Taylor Fry Case Competition 2026.pdf` (16-slide brief, October 2026). Other files in folder: `beach_monthly.csv`, `incidents.csv`, `pricing.csv`, `Data_dictionary.pdf`, `Marking Guide 2026.xlsx`.

## Background
- Taylor Fryland: fictional coastal country modelled on NSW, millions of beach visitors a year.
- Beach risk varies by location (environment, visitor numbers, existing safety measures).
- Government currently funds: lifeguard patrols, drone surveillance, safety education programmes, rescue infrastructure.
- Incidents still occur, especially at beaches with limited safety coverage.
- All data is fictional, for learning only.

## Client objective
- Government is considering an **extra $2M per year** in beach safety funding.
- Goal: an evidence-based strategy that **minimises the number of severe beach incidents** across all beaches.
- Output informs next year's funding priorities and is presented to **non-technical government officials**.

## Tasks
**Task 1 – Forecast demand (next year's incidents)**
1. Estimate expected number of incidents **by severity** across beaches next year; identify **where and when** they are most likely.
2. Assess **factors influencing incidents and severe incidents**: beach conditions plus existing safety measures (lifeguards, patrol hours, drone surveillance).

**Task 2 – Allocate the extra $2M/yr**
1. Using the data and Task 1, recommend allocation **across beaches and safety initiatives** to minimise severe incidents.
2. Explain why this is the best use of public funds: key **implementation considerations** and **why alternatives were not selected**.

## Data provided
1. **Beach_monthly** – monthly data for **80 beaches**, 01/09/2016–31/08/2026: beach characteristics, visitor volumes, environmental conditions, safety resources.
2. **Incidents** – incident-level records, same period: cause, severity, visitor characteristics, response information.
3. **Pricing** – costs and implementation info for beach safety initiatives.

Must **explicitly state assumptions** and **limitations** that may affect the analysis.

## Submission
- 10-minute presentation as a **slide pack**.
- Semi-finalists and finalists present to a panel of ASOC and Taylor Fry representatives, followed by Q&A.
- Email to careers@asoc.unsw.edu.au, subject `Taylor Fry Case Competition – [Team Name]`; team name and members in the email body.
- Deadline: as specified by UNSW ASOC.

## Assessment criteria
| Weight | Criterion |
|---|---|
| 45% | Task 1 – explanation and justification of analysis (clear derivation, reasonable analysis, well-justified conclusions) |
| 20% | Task 2 – explanation and justification of analysis |
| 10% | Tasks 1 & 2 – clarity of solutions (estimating incidents, proposed interventions) |
| 25% | Style of presentation (effective slides, engaging and coherent speakers, thoughtful Q&A responses) |

## Tips from the brief
- AI use is permitted, but problem-solving and clear evidence of reasoning are expected; heavy reliance on AI output "will be obvious".
- Be concise, self-contained, clear; avoid unnecessary elaboration.
- Prepare for Q&A on qualitative/quantitative insights, technical implementation, overall approach; justify decisions and methodology.
- No single correct answer; creativity, logical reasoning and well-rounded understanding are rewarded. No fourth-year-level methods expected.
- Answer the question asked; don't go beyond scope.
- **No external research or datasets**: use only the information provided; make reasonable assumptions where unspecified.
- Any software allowed (R, Python, Excel, MATLAB, etc.).
- Finalists get feedback after the semi-final and may submit a revised submission.

## Prizes
- Winner: $200 voucher (single or split among team) + fast-track past the first shortlisting phase of Taylor Fry graduate recruitment (in the year of applying).
- Runner-up: $100 voucher.
